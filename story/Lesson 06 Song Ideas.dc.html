LESSON 06 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

Flood and Babel: The Unraveling

Eight directions on the hardest stretch of Genesis. The songs here have to carry violence honestly without enjoying it — and the strongest of them is a single number: the arithmetic Lamech uses for revenge and Jesus takes back for forgiveness.

SOURCE · LESSON 06 · CAIN, LAMECH, THE FLOOD, COVENANT, BABEL, PENTECOST

THEOLOGICAL CENTER
God keeps placing limits on human violence
EMOTIONAL CENTER
Exhaustion with the cycle — and relief that it can stop
AVOID
Flood imagery as cheerful children's song
SONG IDEA 01 · THE STRONGEST IDEA IN THE LESSON
Seventy-Seven
DARK VERSES · HUGE CHORUS
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Lamech multiplies vengeance; Jesus uses the same number to multiply forgiveness. Almost no Christian song makes this connection.
HOOK
Seventy-seven for the wound
Seventy-seven for the score
We kept multiplying vengeance
Till we couldn't count anymore
THE TURN
Then You said seventy-seven
But You meant forgive again
You took the number violence owned
And gave it back to love instead
VERSES
Verses in Lamech's voice — boastful, rhythmic, unpleasant — and do not soften them; the reversal only lands if the boast has weight. Then hold the same number, same meter, and change only the verb. The whole song is one word doing two jobs.
KEY IMAGERY
Numbers · a tally · blood debt · a clenched fist becoming an open hand.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 4:23–24 · Matthew 18:21–22
SONG IDEA 02 · BABEL AS ORGANIZED FEAR
What Fear Builds
ATMOSPHERIC · CINEMATIC
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Babel as organized fear: humanity builds systems of permanence, identity and control because it cannot tolerate vulnerability.
HOOK
Look what fear can build
Towers in the sky
Walls around a fragile heart
Tall enough to hide
CHORUS
We made a name
We made a wall
We promised we'd be safe from falling
Brick by brick
We climbed above
Everything we were afraid of
THE LINE IT TURNS ON
But nothing built by fear
Can teach a heart to trust
VERSES
Move outward in scale — a person, a family, a company, a nation — with the same brick imagery each time, so the listener hears the pattern rather than being told about it. Resist naming any modern institution; the song is stronger when the listener supplies it.
KEY IMAGERY
Bricks · tower · walls · city lights · a name carved in stone.
ADDED REFRAIN
Fear can build a city
Fear can build a wall
Fear can build an empire
It just can't make us safe at all
KEEP THIS
Probably the single strongest four lines in the sheet — protect them through every revision. They also carry a recurring course motif: fear can build an empire, it cannot make us safe.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 11:1–9
SONG IDEA 03
Filled With Violence
PROPHETIC · HEAVY · URGENT
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Genesis diagnoses the earth as filled with violence — hamas, the violence the strong do to the weak. Systemic, not merely individual.
REFRAIN
Filled with violence
From the brother to the throne
Every wound becomes another
Till nobody knows who started it
THE TURN
Someone has to stop the story
Someone has to take the blow
If revenge keeps making children
Of the hatred that we sow
VERSES
“Someone has to take the blow” is doing atonement work — let it stay unresolved here rather than explaining it. The cross is the answer this song is not yet allowed to give.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 6:11 · Matthew 5:38–44
SONG IDEA 04 · NOBODY WRITES THIS ONE
The Mark of Cain
REFLECTIVE · STRANGE
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Even Cain, after murder, is marked in a way that limits retaliatory violence. The mark is protection, not branding — and most listeners have it backwards.
HOOK
You marked the murderer
But not for death
You drew a line around revenge
Before we knew the word forgiveness
You were teaching us where violence ends
VERSES
Sit inside the strangeness rather than resolving it: the first thing God does after the first murder is protect the murderer. Do not soften Abel's blood — the song has to hold both, and its restraint is the theology.
KEY IMAGERY
A mark · blood crying from the ground · a boundary line drawn in dust.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 4:10–15
SONG IDEA 05 · THE COVENANT SONG
After the Flood, a Promise
TENDER · RESTRAINED
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Judgment is not the final word; covenant and restraint follow. And the covenant is with every living creature, asking nothing in return.
HOOK
After the flood, a promise
After the storm, a bow
You tied Yourself to creatures
Who still didn't know You well
THE TURN
We promised nothing
You promised first
To hold a world that still could hurt
VERSES
This is the one song that keeps the whole set from being about violence. Keep the animals in it — the covenant's reach is the point. The sign is a weapon hung up in the sky; if that image can be carried without explanation, use it.
KEY IMAGERY
Rain · a waterline on stone · rainbow · animals · open sky.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 9:8–17
SONG IDEA 06 · CONNECTS TO THE GRACE / BELONGING THREAD
Make a Name
INTIMATE · ACCESSIBLE
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Babel's desire to “make a name for ourselves” as the search for identity apart from received belonging.
HOOK
We couldn't trust the name You gave
So we carved our own in stone
Built it high enough for heaven
Just to prove we weren't alone
CHORUS
I don't need to make a name
If You already know mine
I don't need a tower
To be seen from where You are
VERSES
Babel at civilizational scale in 02; Babel inside one person here. The pairing is deliberate — if both get written, they belong in the same set, in that order.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 11:4 · Isaiah 43:1
SONG IDEA 07 · BABEL → PENTECOST
Scattered but Not Lost
HOPEFUL · COMMUNAL
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Babel's scattering is not the end. Pentecost later turns difference into communion without erasing it.
REFRAIN
Scattered but not lost
Different tongues, the same breath
You never asked us all to sound alike
To make us one again
THE LINE
Babel feared the difference
Pentecost let difference sing
VERSES
Worth arranging for more than one language, if the congregation has them — the form can carry the theology directly. Keep Acts' actual list of peoples somewhere in it; the specificity is what makes the point unsentimental.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 11 · Acts 2:1–12
SONG IDEA 08 · THE DEVELOPMENTAL-LAW FRAMEWORK
Stop the Escalation
FOLK / AMERICANA · TEACHING SONG
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The developmental movement from revenge to restraint to forgiveness — the framework we need in place before Sinai.
HOOK
Before I learn to love my enemy
Teach my hand to stay
Before mercy fills my heart
Put a boundary in the way
CHORUS
First stop the escalation
Then teach the heart another road
Law can hold the violence
Till love can carry what it holds
VERSES
A teaching song, and it should sound like one — plain, unhurried, no swell. The danger is smugness; keep it in first person and let the singer be the one who needs the boundary.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 21:23–25 · Matthew 5:38–44

Connects Lamech and Jesus in a way almost no Christian song does.

Preserve as a recurring curriculum phrase: Restraint is not the destination. It can be the scaffolding that makes a deeper morality possible.
