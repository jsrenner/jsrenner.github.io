THE CHRISTIAN STORY · LESSON 09 SONG IDEAS SHEET
LESSON 09 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

Sinai, Conquest, Judges: Covenant and Violence

Ten directions, and the hardest lesson to sing so far. The trajectory is the material: Lamech multiplies revenge, Torah caps it, the prophets ask for mercy, Jesus relinquishes it. Songs here should teach that movement without pretending the early stages were already the destination.

SOURCE · LESSON 09 · COVENANT, RESTRAINT, CONQUEST, INTERNALIZATION

THEOLOGICAL CENTER
Structure serves transformation
EMOTIONAL CENTER
Being taught slowly, at the speed we can bear
AVOID
Old Testament bad / Jesus good
THE PROGRESSION EVERY SONG HERE SHOULD SERVE
Lamech: unlimited revenge → Torah: limit revenge → Prophets: justice and mercy → Jesus: relinquish revenge and love the enemy
SONG IDEA 01
The Container
REFLECTIVE · GRADUAL BUILD
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Structure can make transformation possible without being the destination.
CHORUS
The container isn't the kingdom
The rule isn't the reason
Build the walls until my heart
Can hold what they were teaching
Give me structure while I'm learning
Till the law becomes my way
Then write the words inside me
Where love no longer has to be explained
VERSES
Scaffolding imagery, held honestly: it is real, it bears weight, nobody moves into it. Verse one is the boundary that keeps a person from harm; verse two is the day the boundary is no longer what stops them.
KEY IMAGERY
Scaffolding · stone tablet · trellis · walls · handwriting.
SCRIPTURE
Jeremiah 31:31–34 · Mark 2:27 · Romans 13:8–10
SONG IDEA 02 · STRONGEST CONCEPT
From Lamech to Jesus
NARRATIVE · CINEMATIC
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The whole biblical moral trajectory in one song.
OPENING
Lamech said seventy-seven
Every wound deserves a war
Moses drew a boundary
You don't get to take one more
SECOND VERSE
Prophet said I want mercy
More than blood upon the stone
Jesus said love your enemy
And let the vengeance go
CHORUS
Maybe You were teaching us slowly
What our fear couldn't understand
First unclench the fist by a measure
Then open up the hand
NOTE
One stage per verse, in order, no commentary. The song does the teaching the lesson's trajectory visual does — which makes it the most curriculum-specific piece in the set.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 4:23–24 · Exodus 21:23–25 · Micah 6:8 · Matthew 5:38–44
SONG IDEA 03
Write It on My Heart
WORSHIPFUL · SPECIFIC
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
External law becomes internalized love.
HOOK
Don't leave it on the stone
Don't leave it on the page
Write it somewhere deeper
Than the fear that makes me behave
CHORUS
Write it on my heart
Till mercy feels like breathing
Till I don't need a boundary
To tell me not to wound
Write it on my heart
Till love becomes the reason
And everything the law was reaching for
Comes alive in You
VERSES
Keep “the fear that makes me behave” — it names honestly where formation starts, which is what saves the song from being generic. Do not let the verses despise the boundary; they are asking to outgrow it.
SCRIPTURE
Jeremiah 31:33 · Matthew 5 · Romans 13:10
SONG IDEA 04 · CARRIES THE RECURRING PHRASE
Already Free
WARM · CONFIDENT
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Torah comes after liberation. Obedience is response, not payment.
HOOK
The stone came after freedom
The command came after sea
You didn't give me rules in Egypt
And ask me to earn release
CHORUS
Already free
Already chosen
Already carried through
I don't obey to make You love me
I obey because You do
BRIDGE
I don't have to do to get
I already have it, so I get to do
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 19:3–6 · Exodus 20:2
SONG IDEA 05
One Eye, Not Seventy-Seven
DARK FOLK · AMERICANA
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
“Eye for eye” as restraint, once you compare it with escalating vengeance instead of only with Jesus.
REFRAIN
One eye, not seventy-seven
One wound, don't start a war
It wasn't mercy yet
But maybe mercy needed a door
SECOND SECTION
Stop the blood from multiplying
Hold the anger to a line
Maybe love begins by teaching
Vengeance where it has to die
NOTE
“It wasn't mercy yet” is the whole lesson in four words. Unusual, intellectually strong, and not something a congregation will sing — keep it as a listening song.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 4:23–24 · Exodus 21:23–25 · Matthew 18:21–22
SONG IDEA 06 · THE CONQUEST PROBLEM
God Through a Warrior's Eyes
HAUNTING · UNRESOLVED
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The projection question, put to the war stories — and answered only by Christ as the lens.
HOOK
Every nation had a battle god
Every army said heaven was near
How much of what we called Your anger
Was the echo of our fear?
CHORUS
Show me God through Jesus' eyes
Put every ancient picture in the light
If I called my violence holy
Teach me what I failed to see that night
VERSES
Do not resolve it. The question is asked from inside the tradition, not from outside it — the singer is a believer wanting to see clearly, not a critic scoring points. Origen is the historical warrant for the posture.
SCRIPTURE
Joshua 10:40 · Deuteronomy 20:16–18 · Luke 23:34
SONG IDEA 07 · RECURRING COURSE SONG
A New Vision Doesn't Erase the Old
SPACIOUS · HONEST
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Breakthrough and regression coexist. Development is not a smooth climb.
HOOK
A new vision doesn't erase the old
Yesterday still lives inside tomorrow
Three steps toward the light
Two back into the shadows
CHORUS
We're learning how to see
But learning isn't clean
Grace keeps moving through the places
Where the old world still remains
NOTE
The phrase applies far beyond Torah — it will fit exile, the early church, Constantine and the Reformation. Worth building as a piece the course can return to.
SCRIPTURE
Judges 2:11–19 · Judges 21:25
SONG IDEA 08
Freedom Needs Formation
STRONG · SOCIALLY RESONANT
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Getting out of Egypt is not enough. Free people have to learn freedom.
HOOK
Freedom needs formation
Open doors aren't enough
You can break a chain in a moment
It takes years to learn to love
SECOND SECTION
Teach the rescued not to conquer
Teach the wounded not to wound
Teach the slaves who left a Pharaoh
Not to build another throne
VERSES
Pairs directly with Lesson 08's “Leave Egypt / Get Egypt Out” — that song names the problem, this one asks for the training. Judges is the verse material: the cycle that keeps failing.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 19:3–6 · Judges 2:11–19
SONG IDEA 09 · SETS UP LESSON 11
What Did You Really Want?
QUESTIONING · SPACIOUS
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The question underneath law, ritual and sacrifice — the one the prophets will ask out loud.
CHORUS
Was it blood You really wanted?
Was it rules for rules alone?
Or were all these ancient structures
Teaching hearts a way back home?
SECOND SECTION
Sabbath for the weary
Justice for the poor
Mercy for the stranger
Was that what the law was for?
NOTE
The second section is Torah's own answer, drawn from real commands — which keeps the song from sounding like it is dismissing the law it is questioning.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 20:8–11 · Leviticus 19:33–34 · Micah 6:8
SONG IDEA 10 · ECHOES “PHARAOH IN ME”
Don't Make Me Pharaoh
CONFESSIONAL · PROPHETIC
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Israel has been rescued from domination and is entirely capable of reproducing it.
HOOK
You brought me out of Pharaoh
Now get Pharaoh out of me
Don't let the hands You freed from chains
Become the hands that chain the weak
CHORUS
Teach me freedom that doesn't need
Somebody underneath
Teach me blessing that doesn't rise
By putting someone beneath my feet
NOTE
It will land again at Constantine and at every point where the church holds power. Keep the address personal — the moment it becomes about other people it stops working.
SCRIPTURE
Deuteronomy 15:12–15 · Exodus 22:21–24
SONG IDEA 11 · CONSEQUENCE
Reality Is a Teacher
NARRATIVE · SOBER
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
When instruction is resisted, consequences can eventually reveal what the instruction was trying to teach.
HOOK
You told us where the road would lead
We walked it anyway
Reality became the teacher
When we would not hear Your way
CHORUS
Judges turns the wheel again
Distortion, then the cost
Then the cry, then the rescue
Then a little more is lost
CAREFUL
Do not imply God inflicts suffering in order to teach lessons. The lyric's logic is choices → worlds → consequences → awakening. Innocent people are inside those worlds too, and the song should not sound satisfied.
SCRIPTURE
Judges 2:11–19 · Deuteronomy 30:19
