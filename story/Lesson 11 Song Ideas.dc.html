LESSON 11 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

The Prophets: Justice Before Sacrifice

Twelve directions, and an unusual difficulty: this lesson's material is a critique of worship, aimed at people in the middle of worshipping. That makes several of these songs genuinely uncomfortable to sing — which is the point. The prophets spoke from inside the covenant, and so should these.

SOURCE · LESSON 11 · RITUAL, JUSTICE, INTERNALIZATION, MERCY OVER SACRIFICE

SONG IDEA 01 · CENTRAL SONG OF THE LESSON
Let It Roll
GOSPEL · BUILDING · ANTHEMIC
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Amos 5:24 — justice as water, moving, unstoppable, arriving where it is needed.
HOOK
Take away the noise of my songs
If the songs are all I bring
I don't want an offering
That never learned to change a thing
CHORUS
Let it roll like water
Let it run like rain
Justice down the dry riverbed
Till the valley drinks again
Let it roll, let it roll
Not a trickle we can dam
Let the mercy of the living God
Come flooding through our hands
VERSES
The opening lines have to stay uncomfortable — a congregation singing “take away the noise of my songs” while singing is exactly the prophetic move. Verse two can name the dry riverbed: the specific places where nothing has reached.
KEY IMAGERY
Wadi · flood · dam · drought · water finding the low ground.
SCRIPTURE
Amos 5:21–24
SONG IDEA 02 · THE COURSE'S THESIS IN A CHORUS
Mercy, Not Sacrifice
WARM · CLEAR · TEACHING SONG
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Hosea 6:6, and the fact that Jesus quotes it twice rather than inventing it.
CHORUS
Mercy, not sacrifice
Love, not the smoke of the altar
Not the thing that costs me least
But the thing that makes me alter
Mercy, not sacrifice
Kindness where the ledger ends
You never asked for what I own
You asked me how I treat my friends
VERSES
Verse one from Hosea's mouth, verse two from Jesus' — same sentence, seven centuries apart, in a dispute about eating with the wrong people. The bridge is his accusation: if you had known what this means.
NOTE
“The thing that costs me least” versus “the thing that makes me alter” is the whole theology — sacrifice can be cheaper than change. Do not lose that pair in revision.
SCRIPTURE
Hosea 6:6 · Matthew 9:13 · Matthew 12:7
SONG IDEA 03 · RECURS AT CONSTANTINE & REFORMATION
The Container
SPARE · UNSETTLING
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The religion kept running perfectly after its purpose left.
HOOK
The doors still opened
The songs still rose
The festivals ran like clockwork
And nobody noticed
The container survived
The transformation didn't
SECOND SECTION
A cloak taken for a debt
Spread out beside the altar
Every note in tune
And the poor man in the dust
NOTE
Amos 2:8 gives the image: the seized garment becomes the worship blanket. Do not editorialize it — the juxtaposition is the argument. This song will fit again at Constantine, the Crusades and the Reformation.
SCRIPTURE
Amos 2:6–8 · Isaiah 1:10–17
SONG IDEA 04
Three Things
SIMPLE · SINGABLE · CATECHETICAL
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Micah 6:6–8 — the escalating offer, and the answer with no ritual in it.
VERSE STRUCTURE
Should I come with a calf?
Should I come with a thousand rams?
Rivers of oil, everything I have —
Is that what You demand?
CHORUS
Three things
Only three
Do justice
Love mercy
Walk humbly with your God
Three things, all my life
Every one of them a way to be alive
NOTE
Let the verse escalate absurdly the way Micah does, so the chorus lands as relief rather than as a demand. Strong candidate for the most-used song in the whole curriculum — build it simple enough for a room to learn in one hearing.
SCRIPTURE
Micah 6:6–8
SONG IDEA 05
How Can I Give You Up
TENDER · ACHING · GOD'S VOICE
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Hosea 11 — judgment announced, then interrupted from the inside.
VERSE
I taught you how to walk
I held your arms and let you go
I was the one who bent down
And fed you when you didn't know
REFRAIN
How can I give you up?
How can I hand you over?
My heart turns inside Me
And My anger doesn't win
NOTE
Written in God's voice, which is why it is not congregational — it is a listening song, and probably the most emotionally important piece in the lesson. Heschel is the warrant: the prophets present a God who is affected, not detached. Do not resolve the tension; the text doesn't.
SCRIPTURE
Hosea 11:1–9
SONG IDEA 06 · PAIRS WITH LESSON 09's “WRITE IT ON MY HEART”
Learn to Do Good
PATIENT · HONEST
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Isaiah 1:17 uses the verb learn — justice is a skill, acquired by practice.
HOOK
You didn't say be good
You said learn to do good
Like it's something hands can practice
Like it's something I could
CHORUS
Seek the justice
Plead the widow's case
Defend the ones with no defender
Learn it, learn it, all my days
Not a feeling I discovered
Not a thing I understood
Just the slow apprenticeship
Of learning to do good
NOTE
Gregory of Nyssa's frame in song form: formation is the shape of the life, not a phase before arrival. “Slow apprenticeship” is the phrase to protect.
SCRIPTURE
Isaiah 1:16–17
SONG IDEA 07 · THE PROJECTION QUESTION
You Never Wanted Flattery
QUESTIONING · THEOLOGICAL
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
We may have assumed God wants what kings want: tribute, ceremony, praise.
HOOK
We built You a throne
Because we'd only ever seen a king
Brought You tribute and incense
And called it everything
CHORUS
You never wanted flattery
You wanted us to change
Not the smoke, not the silver
Not the honor of Your name
You wanted the widow fed
You wanted the courtroom clean
All along You wanted something
We were too afraid to be
NOTE
The theologically careful version does not say worship is worthless — it says worship aimed at flattering God has confused God with a monarch. Keep the “we'd only ever seen a king” line; it is the projection question in nine words.
SCRIPTURE
Isaiah 1:11 · Micah 6:7 · Amos 5:23
SONG IDEA 08
The Fast I Choose
DRIVING · CONCRETE
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Isaiah 58 redefines a religious practice by naming what it was meant to produce.
CHORUS
This is the fast I choose:
Loose the chains
Break the yoke
Share the bread
Take them in
Not an empty stomach
But a house with open doors
This is the fast I choose —
Now you know what hunger's for
VERSES
Isaiah's own complaint opens it — why have we fasted and you have not seen? — so the song starts in genuine bafflement rather than in accusation. Note for the group that this is a later text than the eighth-century four.
SCRIPTURE
Isaiah 58:3–10
SONG IDEA 09 · TRAJECTORY SONG
Somewhere Deeper Than a Stone
SPACIOUS · HOPEFUL
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Jeremiah and Ezekiel both locate the answer in the same place: a different interior.
HOOK
You said You'd write it somewhere
Deeper than a stone
Take the rock out of my chest
Give me flesh and blood and bone
CHORUS
Not on tablets
Not on scrolls
Not on doorposts I can pass and never see
Write it where I make my choices
Write it into me
NOTE
The Hebrew lev is closer to the deciding self than to sentiment — “write it where I make my choices” keeps the song from becoming a song about feelings. Both source texts come from exile; flag the dating.
SCRIPTURE
Jeremiah 31:31–34 · Ezekiel 36:26–27
SONG IDEA 10 · THE PRIMARY LESSON 11 SONG
What Did It Make You?
SEARCHING · UNFLINCHING
CONGREGATIONAL WITH CARE
CORE IDEA
The prophetic question turned on the singer: not did I practice, but what did the practice make me?
CHORUS
I kept the feast
I sang the psalm
I knew the words by heart
But what did it make me?
What did it make me?
Tell me what it made me
When nobody was watching
BRIDGE
You can keep every ritual
You can say every line
But nothing I call holy
Gets to make a lie divine
ALTERNATE BRIDGE LINE
Reality is patient
But it never learns to lie
NOTE
“Reality is patient but it never learns to lie” is the strongest single line in the sheet — worth reserving as a recurring motif rather than spending here if a better home appears. Congregational only if the room is prepared for a song that questions itself.
SCRIPTURE
Amos 5:21–24 · Micah 6:6–8
SONG IDEA 11 · CONSEQUENCE
Reality Doesn't Bend
PROPHETIC · DARK · RESTRAINED
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Worship, certainty and religious identity cannot make injustice sustainable or falsehood true.
HOOK
Sing it louder if you want to
Bring another sacrifice
But the song can't turn oppression
Into something just and right
CHORUS
Reality doesn't bend
Just because I call it holy
Truth can wait outside the temple
Till the walls admit the lie
NOTE
Restraint is essential. Sung with anger it becomes a song about other people's hypocrisy; sung quietly it stays prophetic. “Truth can wait outside the temple” is the image to build the arrangement around.
SCRIPTURE
Isaiah 1:10–17 · Amos 5:21–24 · Jeremiah 7:4
SONG IDEA 12 · CONSEQUENCE, WITH THE VICTIM NUANCE INTACT
The Road We Built
SOMBRE · HONEST
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Prophetic judgment as seeing where present choices are already leading.
HOOK
We kept asking why You sent us here
Maybe this is where the road went
Every stone was laid in freedom
Every warning came and went
SECOND SECTION
Not every wound was earned
Not every child chose the road
But the world we build together
Leaves a weight for someone else to hold
NON-NEGOTIABLE
The second section must stay. Without it the song becomes a theology of deserved suffering, which this curriculum explicitly rejects. Empires create victims; children carry consequences they had no part in choosing.
SCRIPTURE
Amos 2:6–8 · Hosea 11:1–9 · Lamentations 2:11

RECURRING LYRICAL MOTIFS · ACROSS THE WHOLE COURSE
“Reality can be resisted. It cannot be made false.”
“Freedom chose the road. Reality showed where it went.”
“Fear can build an empire. It cannot make us safe.”
“You didn't need the wreckage, but You would not leave it dead.”
“Consequence is not destiny.”
“Love can be resisted without being defeated.”

Use these selectively so listeners begin recognising the thread rather than the phrase. And protect one distinction above all as the course moves toward the cross: God bringing good out of suffering is not the same as God needing the suffering in order to bring the good.
