LESSON 02 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

Before Israel: The Ancient Religious World

This lesson's songs live at the projection question: humanity reaching upward, building God in its own image, and a God who comes down instead. Confessional before it is worshipful — the redemption has to be earned inside the song.

SOURCE · LESSON 02 (REVISED) · PROJECTION, REACHING, DESCENT

THEOLOGICAL CENTER
We climb; God descends
EMOTIONAL CENTER
Repentance for the god we made
AVOID
Mocking ancient religion
SONG IDEA 01
We Returned the Favor
CONFESSIONAL · HAUNTING
THEN REDEMPTIVE
CORE IDEA
God made humanity in God's image; humanity keeps making God in ours. The projection question, sung as confession rather than accusation — the singer is inside the indictment.
HOOK / REFRAIN
You made us in Your image
We returned the favor
Gave You all our borders
All our kings and wars
Called our fear Your anger
Called revenge Your justice
Then You came among us
And showed us who You are
CHORUS
Jesus, break the mirror
Show me what is true
Strip away the god I made
Until I can see You
VERSES
Verse 1 in the ancient world — a storm god who wants what the king wants. Verse 2 moves to the present without changing the sentence structure, so the listener notices it is the same offense: our nation's god, our party's god, our god who happens to hate what we hate. The turn is not our insight but the incarnation: the chorus can only arrive after the mirror is named. Keep the pre-chorus quiet enough that "break the mirror" lands as a plea, not a slogan.
KEY IMAGERY
Mirror · carved and painted faces · borders drawn on a map with a god on each side · a face that will not fit the frame we built.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 1:26–27 · Isaiah 44:9–20 · Psalm 50:21 · John 14:9 · Colossians 1:15
SONG IDEA 02
The God Who Meets Us
WORSHIPFUL · CINEMATIC
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Every ancient religion was a way of climbing. The biblical claim runs the other direction: God comes toward humanity, and arrives where we already are rather than where we should be.
HOOK / REFRAIN
We built the towers
We lit the fires
We brought the offering
We climbed toward heaven
But You were already coming down
CHORUS
You are the God who meets us
In the dust and in the fear
While we're reaching through the darkness
You are already near
VERSES
Build the verses as ascent — ziggurat stairs, smoke, a shrine at the summit — with the music rising and thinning as it climbs. Then drop, don't lift, into the chorus: the melodic high point should be the descent, not the climb. Verse 3 can name the descent's endpoints in order: a tent in a wilderness, a manger, a cross. Treat the ancient builders with respect; their reaching was real.
KEY IMAGERY
Ziggurat stairs · smoke going up, rain coming down · a tent pitched among tents · a hand reaching up meeting a hand already lowered.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 11:1–9 · Exodus 3:7–8 · John 1:14 · Philippians 2:6–8 · Acts 17:27
SONG IDEA 03 · MOST UNUSUAL
History Shows the Reaching
THEOLOGICAL POETRY
ATMOSPHERIC · NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Religion is nearly universal. History can show us the reaching; it cannot settle what the reaching means. The song holds the question honestly instead of resolving it — a rare thing for a Christian song to do.
HOOK / REFRAIN
History shows the reaching
Hands raised into the dark
Altars, temples, prayers and questions
Searching for a spark
Were we only naming shadows
Or answering a call?
Were we reaching out for heaven
While Heaven reached for us all?
VERSES
Catalogue verses, each a real place and practice, sung without judgment: Göbekli Tepe's raised stones, Egyptian sun-boats, Ur's household gods, Canaanite grain offerings, a mother's prayer in a language no one now speaks. Only the last verse lets a Christian claim in, and even then as testimony rather than proof: this is what I have come to believe the reaching was. The two questions in the refrain must stay questions — no bridge that answers them.
KEY IMAGERY
Hands raised into darkness · standing stones older than farming · shadows on a cave wall · one candle in an empty temple · a spark that may or may not have been struck from outside.
SCRIPTURE
Acts 17:22–28 · Romans 1:19–20 · Ecclesiastes 3:11 · Psalm 82 · Joshua 24:2
STILL UNMINED · LINES WORTH KEEPING
“Abraham's father kept other gods; You started there anyway.”
“Mercy, not the smoke” — Hosea 6:6 as a whole refrain.
“Every god we build gets hungry.”
