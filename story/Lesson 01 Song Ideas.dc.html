LESSON 01 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

What Kind of Book Is the Bible?

Three song concepts drawn from this lesson's theology: revelation unfolding through many voices, the human fingerprints on Scripture, and Christ as the interpretive center. These are starting points, not finished songs — the strongest lyrical possibilities the lesson actually contains.

SOURCE · LESSON 01 (REVISED) · GENRE, HUMAN VOICE, CHRIST AS CENTER

THEOLOGICAL CENTER
Revelation unfolds; we learn to see
EMOTIONAL CENTER

AVOID
Bible-as-rulebook praise
SONG IDEA 01
Learning to See
REFLECTIVE · EXPANSIVE
CINEMATIC
CORE IDEA
The Bible is not a flat rulebook but a library of people wrestling with God across centuries. The unity is not uniformity — it is a long, uneven education in how to see.
HOOK / REFRAIN
We were learning how to see
Through the pages, through the questions
Through the things we got wrong
You were teaching us to see
VERSES
One genre per verse, moving forward in time: law given to a freed slave people; poetry that dares to accuse God; prophets shouting into politics; lament with no resolution; then a Gospel — a life, not a rule. Each verse ends with the same half-line, so the shape itself argues that revelation accumulates. Final verse turns to the singer: and now it comes to us.
KEY IMAGERY
Light arriving slowly rather than all at once · a room seen first by lamp, then by dawn · many hands on one long scroll · a face gradually coming into focus.
SCRIPTURE
Hebrews 1:1–3 · Psalm 23 · Lamentations 3:19–24 · Luke 1:1–4 · 2 Corinthians 3:18
SONG IDEA 02
The Wrestling Is the Story
INTIMATE · QUESTIONING
HOPEFUL
CORE IDEA
The tensions and human fingerprints in Scripture may not be defects to explain away; they may be part of how revelation actually happens. God does not seem to require us to be finished before speaking to us.
HOOK / REFRAIN
Maybe the wrestling is the story
Maybe the questions belong
Maybe You meet us in the searching
And stay when we get You wrong
VERSES
Verse 1: two proverbs that contradict each other, and the relief of admitting it. Verse 2: Jacob at the river — a name given in the middle of a fight, a limp carried afterward. Verse 3: the singer's own unresolved question, named plainly and not answered. Resist a bridge that resolves it; let the chorus's maybe hold. The word "maybe" should never be sung ironically.
KEY IMAGERY
Night river · a hip that never healed right · margins full of other people's handwriting · a door held open from the inside.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 32:24–31 · Proverbs 26:4–5 · Psalm 88 · Job 38–42 · Mark 9:24
SONG IDEA 03 · STRONGEST CANDIDATE
Read It Through Your Face
WORSHIPFUL · CHRIST-CENTERED
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Christ is the interpretive center of Scripture. When passages pull against each other, Christians do not average them — we read them toward the face of Jesus.
HOOK / REFRAIN
When the words are hard to hold
When the pages pull apart
Let me read them through Your face
Let me see them through Your heart
VERSES
Verse 1 names the hard text honestly without quoting it as a slogan — a page the singer would rather skip. Verse 2 is the road to Emmaus: two people walking with the interpreter and not knowing it yet. A final short verse can hold only one line — no one has ever seen God; the Son has made him known — sung nearly unaccompanied. Keep the melody singable by a room; this is the one on the sheet a congregation could actually carry.
KEY IMAGERY
A face as a lens · Emmaus road at dusk · a page held up to light · the veil lifting.
SCRIPTURE
John 1:18 · Luke 24:25–27 · John 5:39–40 · Colossians 1:15 · Matthew 5:21–48
