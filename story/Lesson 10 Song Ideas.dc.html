LESSON 10 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

Kings: David and the Cost of a Crown

Nine directions. This lesson is unusually singable because its material is human rather than abstract: fear, the wish for someone strong enough to fix things, and what happens to a person who can simply send. Two things to protect — the repeated verb take, and the refusal to make David either a villain or a saint.

SOURCE · LESSON 10 · SECURITY, POWER, ACCOUNTABILITY, KINGSHIP TRANSFORMED

THEOLOGICAL CENTER
Power amplifies; the crown moves toward the cross
EMOTIONAL CENTER
Wanting someone strong enough to make us unafraid
AVOID
Partisan politics · David as cautionary villain
KEEP AS RECURRING COURSE LANGUAGE
We came out of Egypt. Egypt didn't always come out of us.
Not so among you.
Power does not eliminate human brokenness — it amplifies it.
SONG IDEA 01 · MOST DISTINCTIVE TO THIS LESSON
The King Will Take
DARK · RHYTHMIC · PROPHETIC
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Samuel's warning, with its one repeated verb doing all the work.
OPENING
Give us somebody strong enough
To fight the things we fear
Give us someone we can see
And put the power here
REFRAIN
The king will take your sons
The king will take your daughters
The king will take your fields
Till the thing that makes you safer
Owns everything you built
CHORUS
We asked for someone strong enough
To save us from our fear
Never asked what power costs
When all of it gathers here
ADDED BRIDGE
We were free to choose the kingdom
Couldn't choose what kingdoms do
Freedom chose the crown
History showed the cost
NOTE
Let the refrain accumulate — the text repeats the verb six times and the song should feel that pile-up. The last line of the chapter is “and you will be his servants”; consider ending there without comment.
SCRIPTURE
1 Samuel 8:10–18
SONG IDEA 02
You Are the Man
SPARSE · DRAMATIC · STORY-SONG
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Nathan confronting David: truth can speak to power.
VERSE
A rich man had a thousand sheep
A poor man only one
You heard the story from your throne
And knew what should be done
THEN EVERYTHING DROPS
You are the man.
CHORUS
When I can see another's wrong
But somehow cannot see my own
Send a Nathan through the door
And don't let power leave me alone
NOTE
Musically the whole song exists to set up four words. Whatever the arrangement, everything must clear out of the way for them. The chorus turns the accusation toward the singer, which is what keeps it from being smug.
SCRIPTURE
2 Samuel 12:1–7
SONG IDEA 03 · MAJOR CHRIST SONG OF THE CURRICULUM
The Crown and the Cross
BIG · CINEMATIC · CHRIST-CENTERED
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Jesus inherits Davidic kingship by transforming what kingship means.
HOOK
David wore a crown of gold
You wore a crown of thorn
Kings kept asking who would serve them
You knelt down upon the floor
CHORUS
The crown became a cross
The throne became a tree
Power opened up its hands
And gave itself away for me
BRIDGE
Not so among you
Not the way the kingdoms do
If you want to be the greatest
Learn to kneel and serve the few
NOTE
Keep the inheritance language — he does not abandon the throne, he redefines it. “Son of David” is a claim, not a demotion.
SCRIPTURE
Mark 10:42–45 · John 13 · Matthew 27:29
SONG IDEA 04 · RECURS AT CONSTANTINE & CRUSADES
Not So Among You
SIMPLE · MEMORABLE
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Jesus directly rejects domination as the pattern of leadership.
CHORUS
Not so among you
Not the way the rulers reign
Not by making others smaller
Not by fear and not by pain
If you want to know My kingdom
Watch Me kneel and wash their feet
Not so among you
Power looks different with Me
NOTE
Strong candidate for a recurring phrase later at Constantine, the Crusades, church authority and political Christianity. Simple enough to reprise as a fragment inside other songs.
SCRIPTURE
Mark 10:42–45 · John 13:1–17
SONG IDEA 05 · DEEPEST RECURRING IDEA IN THE PROJECT
We Came Out of Egypt
PROPHETIC · HEAVY · UNSETTLING
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Liberated people can recreate the systems they escaped.
HOOK
We came out of Egypt
Egypt didn't leave us
Broke the chains around our hands
Then built them into kingdoms
CHORUS
Every Pharaoh starts somewhere
Every throne can learn to take
We escaped the house of bondage
Then rebuilt it in another shape
KEY IMAGERY
Chains becoming crowns · pyramids becoming palaces · slaves becoming overseers.
NOTE
The concept recurs at Constantine and the institutional church. Preserve this line for reuse across the whole course: We came out of Egypt. Egypt didn't always come out of us.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 1 · 1 Samuel 8 · 1 Kings 4–12
SONG IDEA 06 · CONNECTS TO LESSON 06 BABEL
Enough Power to Be Safe
INTROSPECTIVE · NOT POLITICAL
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The search for control is usually fueled by fear.
REFRAIN
How much power would be enough
To finally make me unafraid?
How high would every wall have to rise
Before I felt okay?
SECOND SECTION
Babel had a tower
Solomon had a throne
I have my own little kingdoms
I build so I won't feel alone
NOTE
The third verse is the one that makes it work — keep the scale personal. Once the song is about other people's kingdoms it stops being honest and starts being commentary.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 11 · 1 Kings 4:20–28
SONG IDEA 07
He Saw, He Sent, He Took
VERY DARK · MURDER-BALLAD
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
David's abuse of Bathsheba and Uriah, with the verbs echoing Samuel's warning that the king will take.
VERSES
He saw
He sent
He took
And nobody told the king no

He hid
He planned
He signed
And one good man didn't come home
CHORUS
Power makes a quiet room
When everyone is scared to speak
Till a prophet walks through the door
And tells the strong man he is weak
NOTE
Closer to Americana narrative than worship music, and that is correct for this material. “Power makes a quiet room” is the strongest line — it names the real mechanism of the chapter.
SCRIPTURE
2 Samuel 11 · 1 Samuel 8:10–18
SONG IDEA 08
Promise and Warning
REFLECTIVE · MATURE · GRACIOUS
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
David embodies both genuine divine promise and human failure.
HOOK
You were promise
You were warning
Shepherd boy and broken king
God could work through all your weakness
Without calling weakness clean
CHORUS
The story didn't hide you
Didn't paint the shadows gold
Grace can keep a promise
Without pretending wrong is whole
NOTE
This is Chrysostom's reading set to music: David's standing rests on repentance and mercy, not on innocence. “Without calling weakness clean” is the theological hinge — do not lose it in revision.
SCRIPTURE
2 Samuel 7 · 2 Samuel 11–12 · Psalm 51
SONG IDEA 09 · THE CONSCIOUSNESS-SHIFT PHRASE
A King Who Needs Saving
STORY-DRIVEN · GRADUALLY CHRIST-CENTERED
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
A king to save us → a king who needs saving → a different kind of King.
REFRAIN
We wanted a king to save us
Found a king who needed saving
Put our hope upon a throne
Found the same old fear there waiting
SECOND SECTION
Every crown sits on a human
Every kingdom bears the scar
Maybe what we needed wasn't
Just a stronger version of who we are
LAST TURN
Till a different King came walking
Without an army, without a sword…
NOTE
“Just a stronger version of who we are” is the line worth building the whole arrangement around. Let the last turn arrive late and stay understated.
SCRIPTURE
1 Samuel 8:4–22 · Mark 10:42–45
