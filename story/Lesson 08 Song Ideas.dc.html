LESSON 08 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

Exodus: The God Who Takes Sides

Nine directions. This is the lesson where the course's spine — rescue before requirement — becomes singable. Two things to protect: the order (cry, hearing, liberation, covenant, law) and the honesty (liberation does not make us incapable of becoming Pharaoh).

SOURCE · LESSON 08 · CRY, HEARING, PASSOVER, LIBERATION, FORMATION

THEOLOGICAL CENTER
Rescue first. Formation second.
EMOTIONAL CENTER
A cry from the brick pits is heard
AVOID
“Those bad Egyptians” · God as tribal mascot
KEEP AS RECURRING COURSE LANGUAGE
Rescue first. Formation second.

SONG IDEA 01 · CENTRAL CURRICULUM SONG
Rescue First
STRONG · ANTHEMIC
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Israel is liberated before it receives Torah. The order is the theology.
HOOK
You didn't hand us laws in Egypt
And tell us how to earn the sea
You broke the chains before the mountain
Then taught the rescued how to be free
CHORUS
Rescue first
Then teach my feet to walk
Grace before the road
Love before the law
I don't have to do to get
I already have it, so I get to
Live like someone You set free
VERSES
Verse one in Egypt — bricks, quotas, no promise in sight. Verse two on the far shore, sea behind, mountain ahead, law not yet given. Let the bridge name what obedience is for: not entry, but formation.
KEY IMAGERY
Chain · sea · mountain · open road · empty hands.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 2–20 · Exodus 20:2
SONG IDEA 02
God Heard
SLOW · DIRECT
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Exodus begins because suffering is noticed. Salvation begins with being seen.
HOOK
We thought the bricks had buried us
Too deep for heaven to know
Then somewhere in the silence
God heard
CHORUS
God heard
God saw
God knew what Pharaoh called normal
God heard
God came near
The cry was never wasted in the air
VERSES
Build the verses out of the four verbs of Exodus 2:24–25 — heard, remembered, saw, knew. Resist adding a rescue until the last verse; the song's claim is that attention comes first.
KEY IMAGERY
Brick pits · night cries · dust · unseen tears.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 2:23–25 · Exodus 3:7–8
SONG IDEA 03 · DEVELOPMENTAL CENTER
Leave Egypt / Get Egypt Out
GOSPEL-BLUES · SOULFUL
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Liberation is an event; becoming free is a process.
HOOK
It took one night to leave Egypt
It took a lifetime to get Egypt out
Chains can fall from hands in a moment
But fear knows how to stay around
CHORUS
Lead me farther than the border
Farther than the sea
Don't just take me out of bondage
Take the bondage out of me
VERSES
Use the wilderness complaints honestly — the people remember Egypt's food, not its whips. That is how formation works: the imagination lags behind the rescue.
KEY IMAGERY
Border · wilderness · old habits · empty pots · long road.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 14–17 · Numbers 11
SONG IDEA 04
Pharaoh in Me
CONFESSIONAL · DARK
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Being rescued from Pharaoh does not mean we can never reproduce Pharaoh's consciousness.
HOOK
I know how to name the Pharaoh
When the chains are on my hands
Harder when I find his kingdom
Growing in the way I stand
CHORUS
Break the Pharaoh in me
The part that needs control
The fear that turns another life
Into a thing I own
VERSES
Keep the accusation inward the whole way through. Pharaoh's logic — scarcity, fear, control — is the verse material; the singer keeps recognizing it in themselves.
KEY IMAGERY
Throne · brick · clenched hand · hardened heart.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 1:8–14 · Deuteronomy 24:17–18
SONG IDEA 05
A God Who Notices
TENDER · PROPHETIC
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The God of Exodus sees people who are invisible to empire.
HOOK
Kings saw numbers
You saw names
Pharaoh counted bodies
You heard pain
CHORUS
You are the God who notices
The ones the palace doesn't see
You hear the cry beneath the noise
You come down into slavery
VERSES
Contrast the two vantage points throughout: the palace ledger and the brick pit. The last verse can move to now — who is counted and who is named today.
KEY IMAGERY
Ledger · quota · names · descent · dust.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 3:7–8
SONG IDEA 06 · RECURS AT ROME & CONSTANTINE
What the Palace Calls Order
PROPHETIC FOLK · AMERICANA
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Every empire tells a story about order. Exodus asks who is paying for it.
HOOK
The palace called it order
The brick pits called it pain
One man's peace was built
On another person's chains
CHORUS
Show me who is paying
For the world I call secure
Show me what the throne can't see
From the floor
VERSES
Monuments in verse one, the labor that built them in verse two. Leave the last verse deliberately contemporary and unresolved — the song is a question, not a verdict.
KEY IMAGERY
Monument · scaffolding · straw · marble floor · ledger.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 1:11–14 · Exodus 5:6–19
SONG IDEA 07
The Hardening
DARK · PSYCHOLOGICAL
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Repeated refusal can become character. What we keep choosing can become what we are.
REFRAIN
Every no became another stone
Every closed door became a home
I hardened what I kept defending
Till I couldn't feel the hardening
BRIDGE
Was it You who closed my heart
Or every time I chose to close it?
VERSES
Do not answer the bridge. The text itself keeps both sentences — Pharaoh hardened his heart, and God hardened Pharaoh's heart — and the song should leave the tension standing.
KEY IMAGERY
Stone · closed door · scar tissue · numbness.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 7–14 · Exodus 8:15 · Exodus 9:12
SONG IDEA 08
Passover Means Freedom
CINEMATIC · NARRATIVE
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Before Passover becomes Christian atonement imagery, it is Israel's liberation memory.
HOOK
Before we called it sacrifice
Before we drew the cross
Passover meant slaves walked out
And Pharaoh lost
CHORUS
Passover means freedom
Doors opening in the night
A people walking out of chains
Beneath a borrowed light
VERSES
Stay inside the night of departure: bread with no time to rise, sandals on, a doorway. Let the final verse reach Luke 22 without collapsing the meaning backward into it.
KEY IMAGERY
Doorway · unleavened bread · sandals · night · table.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 12 · Luke 22
SONG IDEA 09 · RETURNS AT EMPIRE & NATIONALISM
Not My Side
PROPHETIC · CHALLENGING
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
God taking the side of the oppressed is not the same as God belonging to my tribe.
HOOK
I keep asking whose side You're on
You keep asking what I've done
To the stranger at my doorway
To the weak beneath my thumb
CHORUS
Maybe You're not on my side
Maybe You're on mercy's side
Maybe every flag I raise
Still has to stand beneath Your light
VERSES
Hold both halves of the lesson's tension: oppression is not morally neutral, and God is not a tribal possession. The prophets judging Israel by Israel's own standard is the second verse.
KEY IMAGERY
Flag · doorway · scales · thumb · borrowed throne.
SCRIPTURE
Exodus 22:21 · Amos 3:1–2 · Amos 9:7
