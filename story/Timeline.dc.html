Everything, happening at once

Israel's story did not happen in a sealed room. While Isaiah was confronting kings in Jerusalem, Homer's poems were taking written shape in Ionia. While the exiles were returning from Babylon, the Buddha was teaching in northern India. While Paul was writing to Corinth, Seneca was writing to Rome about conscience and self-mastery.

Read the columns across, not just down.

A PARTICULAR THEOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

This curriculum reads the parallel columns as more than coincidence: that God was at work in the moral and philosophical awakening of other peoples too, and that the Greek language, Roman roads and Hellenistic thought which shaped the world Jesus entered were not accidents of timing. That is an interpretive conviction, not a historical finding. The dates are the history. What they mean is the argument.

PRIMEVAL STORY GENESIS 1–11 · BEFORE DATED BIBLICAL HISTORY

The opening of the story sits off the date scale.

Creation, Eden, Cain, the flood and Babel occupy the beginning of the biblical narrative, and this timeline deliberately does not assign them years. Genesis 1–11 works as primordial theological narrative rather than dated history: no named kings, no datable events, no archive. From Genesis 12 onward the story narrows to one family and begins to look far more like ordinary ancient history — which is where the dated columns below begin.

Placing these chapters at c. 4000 BC would be inventing a date the text never offers. Leaving them here, before the scale starts, is the more honest reading — and it is not a claim that they matter less.

WHY THIS MATTERS

By the time Jesus is born, the world has been quietly assembled.

A single trade language a fisherman could write in. Roads and sea lanes running from Spain to Syria. Three centuries of Greek philosophy that had already made words like logos, psyche and ethos thinkable. A Jewish diaspora with synagogues in every major city. A Hebrew Bible already translated into Greek, two hundred years early.

None of that was built by Israel. Whether you read it as remarkable historical convergence or as providence is exactly the kind of question this course keeps open — but the convergence itself is not in doubt.
