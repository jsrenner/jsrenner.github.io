LESSON 05 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

The Fall: Where Evil Comes From

Seven directions built from the lesson's real subject: not rule-breaking but mistrust, fear, shame, hiding, blame, and a fractured way of seeing. The most singable idea here is also the most theologically distinctive — God may not have changed; our eyes did.

SOURCE · LESSON 05 · GENESIS 3, PROJECTION, MATURITY, ADAM AND CHRIST

THEOLOGICAL CENTER
The Fall as lost trust, not only broken law
EMOTIONAL CENTER
The ache of hiding from someone safe
AVOID
Shame as a worship aesthetic
SONG IDEA 01 · THE LESSON'S MOST DISTINCTIVE IDEA
What Fear Made of You
HAUNTING · CONFESSIONAL
CONGREGATIONAL WITH CARE
CORE IDEA
Did God become dangerous — or did fear distort humanity's image of God? The projection question, sung.
HOOK
You didn't move
But I started hiding
You didn't change
But fear changed what I saw
I made a monster from the silence
Then blamed You for the walls
CHORUS
Show me what fear made of You
Strip away the face I drew
If You were always coming near
Why did I learn to disappear?
VERSES
Stay first-person throughout — the projection test is for our own images, never a weapon for other people's. Keep the theology exact: God did not become frightening; the lens did. Potentially one of the strongest songs in the whole project.
KEY IMAGERY
Garden · hiding · leaves · shadow · distorted face · mirror.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 3:8–10 · 1 John 4:18
SONG IDEA 02 · MOST HUMANLY ACCESSIBLE
I Was Afraid, So I Hid
QUIET · VULNERABLE
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The Fall as fear, shame and alienation rather than merely rule-breaking.
REFRAIN
I was afraid, so I hid
Covered what You never asked me to cover
I was ashamed, so I ran
From the One who had only been giver
VERSES
Start in Eden, then move into the modern forms hiding takes: performance, religion, success, anger, control. Let the last verse turn toward being found rather than resolving into reassurance too early.
KEY IMAGERY
Fig leaves · locked rooms · a résumé · a raised voice · footsteps in the garden.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 3:10 · Psalm 139
SONG IDEA 03
Did You Really Say?
DARK · TENSE · CINEMATIC
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The serpent's first move is suspicion: can God really be trusted? Mistrust arrives before disobedience.
HOOK
Did You really say?
Are You holding something back?
Is there goodness on the other side
Of everything I lack?
TURN
Every fall begins somewhere
Maybe mine begins right here
When gift becomes suspicion
And love begins to look like fear
VERSES
Write the whole song in the voice of the question rather than answering it — the suspicion is the subject. Resist adding a triumphant last verse; the discomfort is the point, and it makes the next song in a set land harder.
KEY IMAGERY
Whisper · fruit · an open hand becoming a clenched fist · trust unravelling like thread.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 3:1 · Genesis 3:6
SONG IDEA 04 · THE IRENAEAN READING
Good But Not Finished
REFLECTIVE · SPACIOUS
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Irenaeus' developmental view: humanity was created good, but good does not necessarily mean complete.
HOOK
Good but not finished
Loved but still becoming
Born into a garden
With a long way left to grow
CHORUS
Maybe I wasn't made complete
Maybe love was meant to teach me
How to walk before I ran
How to trust before I reached
VERSES
This is the one song on the sheet that lets a congregation exhale. Keep it from becoming self-excusing: immature is not innocent, and the reach was still a reach. Growth, freedom, limits, correction — sung as kindness rather than as permission.
RELATED THEMES
Irenaeus · growth · maturity · freedom · a child who is good without being finished.
SONG IDEA 05 · POSSIBLE COURSE-WIDE ANTHEM
Learning to See Again
HOPEFUL · HEALING
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
If sin is distorted perception, salvation includes the restoration of sight.
HOOK
Maybe salvation is learning to see again
Your face without my fear
My brother without an enemy
This world without scarcity here
CHORUS
Open my eyes
Undo what fear has done
Teach me the world is gift again
Teach me I'm not alone
VERSES
Four verses, four repaired relationships — self, other, creation, God — in the same order Genesis 3 breaks them. This may become a recurring anthem for the whole curriculum; it carries the consciousness theme better than anything else on the sheet.
SCRIPTURE
Mark 8:22–25 · 1 John 4:18 · 2 Corinthians 3:18
SONG IDEA 06
The Fall Was a Lens
POETIC · ATMOSPHERIC
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
The Fall changes how reality is perceived — the same theology as 05, written for a listener rather than a room.
REFRAIN
The fall was more than falling
It was learning how to fear
Looking through a shattered lens
And calling the distortion clear
VERSES
One line each, unhurried: self becomes shame; neighbor becomes threat; God becomes judge; creation becomes scarcity. Then Christ restores vision — and let that final section be musical rather than wordy. Art-rock arrangement; give it room.
KEY IMAGERY
Cracked glass · double exposure · a familiar room in the wrong light.
SONG IDEA 07 · THE LESSON'S CHRIST-CENTERED RESOLUTION
Another Adam
CHRIST-CENTERED · POETIC
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Adam and Christ as two trajectories of humanity — grasping and self-giving.
HOOK
One reached out to take
One opened up His hands
One hid among the trees
One hung upon the wood
TURN
One said, “I was afraid”
One said, “Into Your hands”
Two ways of being human
Two ways to trust the Father
VERSES
The image — one reached for the tree, one gave himself on the tree — is very strong and needs care: state it once, plainly, and never explain it. Paired couplets throughout, Adam's line always first, Christ's always second, so the shape of the song teaches the theology.
SCRIPTURE
Romans 5:12–21 · 1 Corinthians 15:21–22 · Philippians 2:5–8 · Luke 23:46

SONG IDEA 08 · FREEDOM AND REALITY
Against the Grain
SPARE · POETIC
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Human freedom can try to live against the reality of gift, trust and love — but freedom cannot make a false consciousness true.
HOOK
I can run against the grain
Call the gift a thing I earned
Call the fear the truth about me
But reality won't turn
SECOND SECTION
I was free to leave the garden
Never free to make fear true
NOTE
Keep it subtle and poetic — the moment it becomes a lecture about consequences it stops working. The song is a person noticing, not a preacher explaining. Do not let it imply that suffering is deserved.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 3:1–13 · Genesis 2:15–17
