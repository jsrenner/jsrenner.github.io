LESSON 03 · SONGWRITING COMPANION

Genesis: Creation Is Gift

The richest lesson so far for songwriting: gift before requirement, goodness as the first verdict, image rather than projection, and matter as something God has never been against. Four concepts, ranging from congregational to art-rock.

SOURCE · LESSON 03 (REVISED) · GIFT, GOODNESS, IMAGE, MATTER

THEOLOGICAL CENTER
Gift before requirement
EMOTIONAL CENTER
Relief — nothing to prove yet
AVOID
Nature-praise with no theology
SONG IDEA 01 · STRONGEST IN THE PROJECT SO FAR
Before Anything Was Asked
WORSHIPFUL · TENDER
CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Grace precedes obedience, sin, sacrifice and religion. The world is handed over before anyone is asked for anything — and that order is itself the doctrine.
HOOK / REFRAIN
Before anything was asked of me
There was light, there was breath, there was blessing
Before I knew Your name
I woke inside a world already given
CHORUS
Before the law
Before the fall
Before I had a thing to prove at all
You called it good
You gave it free
Grace was here before there was a need
VERSES
Verse 1 stays inside Genesis 1–2: light, water, seed, breath, a garden handed to someone who did nothing to deserve a garden. Verse 2 moves to the singer's own beginning — a body, a language, a name given by someone else, all received before consent. Keep the whole song in the past tense until the last line, then turn present: and it is still being given. Resist adding a repentance bridge; the point is what came first.
KEY IMAGERY
Waking in a room someone else prepared · light before eyes existed to see it · breath given into dust · a table already set · a garden, not a wage.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 1:1–2:3 · Genesis 2:7–15 · Psalm 24:1 · Romans 5:8 · Ephesians 2:8–9
SONG IDEA 02
The First Word Was Good
SIMPLE · DECLARATIVE
WORSHIPFUL
CORE IDEA
The first theological verdict over creation is goodness, not fallenness. Many of us learned the story starting at chapter three; the song puts chapter one back first. Tov means fitting, working, as it should be — not flawless.
HOOK / REFRAIN
The first word wasn't broken
The first word wasn't shame
The first word over everything
Was good before we changed
VERSES
Structure it on the sevenfold verdict of Genesis 1 — six short verses that each end in the same word, then a seventh that lengthens it to very good. The song's argument is entirely in its repetition, so the arrangement should stay bare: one instrument, one voice, a room. A last verse may name the singer's own body or ordinary day as included in that verdict, without arguing for it.
KEY IMAGERY
A verdict spoken aloud over something before it is judged · seven repetitions · morning · ordinary matter named as good.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 1:4, 10, 12, 18, 21, 25 · Genesis 1:31 · Psalm 139:14 · 1 Timothy 4:4
SONG IDEA 03 · BRIDGES LESSONS 02 & 03
Image / Mirror
CONFESSIONAL · PRAYERFUL
SPARE
CORE IDEA
We are made to image God, not to project ourselves onto God. The same verb runs both ways, and only one direction is our vocation. This is the projection question answered from Genesis rather than from confession alone.
HOOK / REFRAIN
I was made to be Your image
Not to make You look like me
Break the mirror I keep building
Teach me how to see
VERSES
Two verses, deliberately parallel and nearly identical in wording — one describing image-bearing, one describing projection — so the listener hears how easily the first becomes the second. Ancient kings set up statues of themselves at the edges of the empire; Genesis says God set up humans. Verse 2 admits the singer has been setting up statues of himself instead. The last line can quote Lesson 01's title phrase, teach me how to see, tying the three lessons together.
KEY IMAGERY
Royal statue at the border of an empire · mirror versus window · a likeness carved backwards · a face turned outward instead of inward.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 1:26–27 · Genesis 5:1–3 · Isaiah 44:13 · 2 Corinthians 3:18 · Colossians 3:10
SONG IDEA 04
Matter Was Never the Enemy
ART-ROCK · CINEMATIC
NOT CONGREGATIONAL
CORE IDEA
Creation is good, and the incarnation intensifies God's relationship with matter rather than correcting it. Against every instinct — ancient and modern — that treats bodies, dirt and time as the problem.
HOOK / REFRAIN
Matter was never the enemy
Dust was always touched by breath
The Word who called the world to being
Would one day enter flesh
VERSES
Long, unhurried verses of physical particulars — clay, salt, blood, bread, wood, water — with no abstraction allowed anywhere in the lyric. Verse 2 names the teachings that called the body a prison and lets them stand as real temptations before answering them. The answer is not an argument but an event: a body, eating fish after resurrection. Structurally this wants to be built on repetition and dynamics rather than a chorus; the refrain can arrive only twice.
KEY IMAGERY
Dust and breath in the same hand · clay under fingernails · bread broken · nail and wood · a risen body eating breakfast.
SCRIPTURE
Genesis 2:7 · John 1:1–14 · Colossians 1:16–17 · Luke 24:39–43 · 1 Timothy 4:4
