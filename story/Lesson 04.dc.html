LESSON 04 WISDOM · LOGOS · THE FULLNESS OF TIME

Christ Before Jesus: Logos and Creation

Christians eventually made an astonishing claim: the Jesus who appears in first-century Galilee was not God's late response to history. The one they called Christ was already there “in the beginning.”

What happens when Jesus is not merely placed inside the story — but at the source of it?

BIBLICAL
Wisdom → Logos → Christ
WHEN
c. 500 BC – AD 100
WHERE
Israel · Alexandria · the Greek World
CONSCIOUSNESS
Fragment · Pattern · Coherence
VOICES OF THE TRADITION
Justin Martyr · Athanasius · Maximus
01 THE STORY

The earliest Christians began with a human life.

Jesus was born. He taught. He healed. He ate with people other people avoided. He challenged power, was executed by the state, and was experienced by his followers as risen.

After Easter the question became much larger than what did he teach?

Who was this person really?

The answers did not stay where we might expect them to stay.

Not simply “a prophet.”
Not simply “a teacher sent by God.”

Christian reflection moved backward through the story. Jesus was connected first with Israel's Messiah. Then with divine Wisdom. Then with the Logos through whom creation itself came to be.

START HERE
A crucified Galilean
THEN
Israel's Messiah
THEN
Divine Wisdom
AND THEN
The Logos of creation

The story moved backward from Jesus to creation.

John rewrites the first sentence of the Bible

John opens his Gospel by deliberately echoing Genesis — and then changes what stands in the opening line.

GENESIS 1:1

“In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.”

JOHN 1:1

“In the beginning was the Logos.”

Two verses later comes the claim that changes the scale of everything.

“All things came into being through him.”

JOHN 1:3
CHRISTIANITY IS NO LONGER SAYING MERELY
God sent Jesus into the world.
IT IS SAYING
The world exists through the one Christians encountered in Jesus.

That is not a modest development of the story. It is a claim about the structure of reality — and it is worth asking how a movement that began with a Galilean carpenter arrived at it.

02 THE WORLD
HISTORICAL FACT

John did not invent the word Logos.

By the first century the term already carried centuries of history — and it had arrived there along more than one road. Three streams matter for this lesson.

ISRAEL · WISDOM

Israel's Scriptures already personify Wisdom as present with God at creation. Proverbs 8 imagines her beside God as the world is ordered — delighting in it.

The Hebrew Bible also imagines creation happening through God's word: God speaks, and reality responds (Psalm 33:6).

GREECE · LOGOS

Greek philosophers were asking a different question: what gives the universe order?

Logos could mean word, speech, reason, account, rational principle, ordering intelligence. Stoic philosophers used it for the reason permeating the cosmos.

Greek thought had developed a vocabulary for an intelligible order beneath reality.

ALEXANDRIA · PHILO

Before Jesus was born, the Jewish philosopher Philo of Alexandria was already bringing these worlds together — reading Israel's Scriptures through Greek categories and using Logos language for God's activity in creation and revelation.

Philo did not believe in Jesus. He shows us that the conceptual bridge already existed before Christianity.

John steps onto a bridge that history had already built.

EXPLORE THIS ERA →

The fullness of time

“When the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son…”

GALATIANS 4:4

Paul calls the moment full — and never says what filled it. So it is worth asking: what if “the fullness of time” was more than a date on a divine calendar? What if several historical streams had been developing at once?

ISRAEL
covenant · prophets · Wisdom · Messiah · resurrection hope
GREECE
Logos · reason · philosophy · universal questions about truth and virtue
ALEXANDRIA
Jewish Scripture in Greek — the Septuagint · Philo · cultural synthesis
ROME · STILL TO COME
roads · imperial connectivity · a common political world

The Old Testament barely tells us what was happening intellectually in Greece. But by the time Jesus appears, Greek language and thought have become crucial to the world in which Christianity will be explained and spread.

The Bible tells Israel's story. It does not tell the whole story of what humanity was thinking.

THEOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

One theological possibility this course will keep exploring is that God was not preparing only Israel. Human consciousness may have been developing in several cultures at once, different peoples discovering partial truths that later became part of the world into which Christ appeared.

That is an interpretation, not something history can demonstrate. We will hold it as a possibility worth taking seriously rather than a conclusion we have proved.

03 THE TEXT

Four passages that make the claim — and one that makes it earliest.

JOHN 1:1–18
The Logos, and then flesh

The prologue moves from “in the beginning” to “all things came into being through him” to the sentence no philosopher had written: “the Word became flesh.”

CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Notice how much of the prologue is Genesis: beginning, light, darkness, life. And notice where John parts company with every Greek use of the word — a rational principle permeating the cosmos does not get born, get tired, weep, or die. Incarnation is where the borrowed vocabulary breaks.

READ JOHN 1:1–14 · JOHN 1:9 · JOHN 1:18
COLOSSIANS 1:15–20
“The image of the invisible God”

An early Christian hymn quoted by Paul: all things created in him, through him and for him — and in him all things hold together.

CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

This is the phrase that ties Lesson 04 back to Lesson 03. Genesis says humanity is made in God's image. Colossians calls Christ the image of the invisible God.

If humanity is meant to image God, and Christ is the clearest image of the invisible God, what might humanity ultimately be meant to become like?

THAT THREAD RUNS THROUGH THE REST OF THE COURSE · READ COLOSSIANS 1:15–20
1 CORINTHIANS 8:6
The earliest version of the claim

“One Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and through whom we exist.” Written roughly twenty-five years after the crucifixion — decades before John.

CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Paul takes Israel's confession that God is one and puts Jesus inside it, with creation running through him. This matters historically: the association of Christ with creation is not a late Greek accretion added centuries later. It is there in the earliest Christian writing we possess.

READ 1 CORINTHIANS 8:6 · COMPARE DEUTERONOMY 6:4
PROVERBS 8:22–31
Wisdom, present at the beginning

Wisdom speaks in her own voice: present before the world, beside God as it is made, rejoicing in it. The Jewish root of the whole idea.

CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Later Jewish writing in Greek pushed this further still — Wisdom of Solomon 7 calls Wisdom a reflection of eternal light and an image of God's goodness, decades before Colossians and Hebrews used almost the same words for Christ.

READ PROVERBS 8:22–31
HEBREWS 1:1–3
Revelation in many modes — and a center

God spoke in many and various ways, and now speaks in a Son — through whom the worlds were made, who is the exact imprint of God's being.

CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

The Greek word behind “exact imprint” is charaktēr — the mark a die leaves in a coin. Hebrews is describing not a resemblance but an impression: what you see in the Son is the shape of the one who pressed it.

READ HEBREWS 1:1–3

04 WORDS THAT MATTER

Three Greek words that carry most of this lesson.

λόγος
LOGOS — WORD / REASON / ORDER
Word, speech, reason, account, or rational principle, depending on context. John applies a word already rich with Jewish and Greek associations to Christ.
CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

The point is not that John simply borrowed Greek philosophy. The point is that he reached for a word capable of speaking into several worlds at once — a Jewish reader hears God's creative speech, a Greek reader hears the order of the cosmos.

And then he says something neither tradition had said: this Logos has a face.

σοφία
SOPHIA — WISDOM
Wisdom. Jewish Wisdom traditions sometimes personify her as present with God in creation.
CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Early Christians heard strong resonances between this language and their claims about Christ — Paul can call Christ “the wisdom of God” directly.

READ PROVERBS 8:22–31
εἰκών
EIKŌN — IMAGE
Image, representation, visible manifestation. Colossians 1:15 calls Christ “the image of the invisible God.”
CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Genesis says humans bear God's image. Colossians identifies Christ as the decisive image of the invisible God. Put those two sentences together and Christians have a way to ask a dangerous, clarifying question:

What does the image of God actually look like? — Look at Christ.

05 THE CONSCIOUSNESS SHIFT
DOCTRINAL CLAIM · HELD ACROSS THE TRADITION
FROM
God sends help into the story
THROUGH
Christ belongs to the story from the beginning
TOWARD
Reality itself has a Christ-shaped coherence

At an earlier stage of faith we might imagine Jesus mainly as God's response to a problem: humanity sins, God sends Jesus. That story is not wrong about the seriousness of what goes wrong. It is simply much smaller than what the New Testament's strongest claims about Christ actually say.

Those claims move much further back. Christ is not the repair technician called after creation breaks. Christ is associated with the source, order and purpose of creation.

Christ is not Plan B.

Put this beside Lesson 03. If creation is gift, and creation comes through the Logos, then grace and Christ both belong to the story before failure ever appears.

Nothing about the Christian story starts with what went wrong.

06 THE CHRIST CONNECTION

Christianity's claim about Christ is not merely that Jesus tells us what God wants. It is that Jesus reveals the pattern of God.

JOHN
The Logos becomes flesh.
COLOSSIANS
Christ is the image of the invisible God.
HEBREWS
He is the exact imprint of God's being.
A CENTRAL INTERPRETIVE CONVICTION OF THIS COURSE

What if Jesus is not simply one revelation of God among many — but the clearest lens through which every other image of God must be tested?

That does not make every earlier biblical image false. It means Christians finally ask a different question of each one: how does this image look when placed beside Christ?

Which brings back a question this course refuses to stop asking.

◇ THE PROJECTION QUESTION · RETURNS

If Christ is the image of the invisible God, what happens to the gods we have made in our own image?

Lesson 02 introduced the danger that humans project our own fear, violence, hierarchy and tribalism onto God. Lesson 04 hands Christianity a test: place the image beside Christ.

DOES OUR GOD LOOK LIKE THIS?
The one who touches lepers, eats with outsiders, forgives enemies, refuses retaliation and gives himself away.
OR MOSTLY LIKE THIS?
An enlarged version of us — our side, our enemies, our anger, our certainty, our hierarchy, at cosmic scale.
THE TEST IS NOT A WEAPON FOR USE ON OTHER PEOPLE'S IMAGES OF GOD. IT IS FIRST FOR OURS.
07 THE TENSION

A difficult question we shouldn't answer too quickly.

Is the preexistent Christ something the apostles discovered — or something the church later read backward into Jesus?

TWO ACCOUNTS THAT MAY NOT CANCEL EACH OTHER
DISCOVERY

The traditional Christian claim is that Jesus really is the eternal Logos, and that the apostles gradually recognized what had always been true.

On this reading, the preexistence of Christ is not invented after Easter. It is perceived after Easter.

CHRISTOLOGICAL REREADING

Historically, early Christians reread Israel's Scriptures after the resurrection and began seeing Christ in texts whose first, immediate context was something else.

Proverbs 8 was not written about Jesus. That is not in serious dispute.

Both observations can stand. The genuinely open theological question is the one underneath them:

Could a later Christ-centered rereading reveal a depth in the earlier text that its first readers did not yet perceive?

We are leaving that open. But notice how well it fits the framework this course is building: revelation can unfold through rereading.

LESSON 01 · JESUS REREADS THE STORY AT EMMAUS · LUKE 24:25–27
THEOLOGICAL / HISTORICAL POSSIBILITY
Think Deeper: Was the world becoming ready?
An idea worth exploring — not something history can prove.
{{ deeperSign }}

Jesus appears at a remarkable historical intersection.

Israel has developed centuries of covenant, prophetic, Wisdom, messianic and resurrection traditions.
Greek philosophy has spent centuries asking about reason, virtue, soul, being and the order of reality.
Greek has become a widely shared language across the eastern Mediterranean.
Jewish Scripture has already been translated into Greek.
Jewish thinkers such as Philo have already begun translating Hebrew theology into Greek philosophical categories.

A few centuries earlier, much of that intellectual and linguistic world would not yet have existed in the same form. A few centuries later, the political and religious landscape would be dramatically different.

What if “the fullness of time” includes a world whose consciousness had become capable of receiving and carrying a new synthesis?

WHAT HISTORY CAN DO
Describe the convergence. The dates, the languages, the translations, the schools, the roads.
WHAT THEOLOGY ASKS
Whether the convergence means anything.

One Christian interpretation is that God was preparing more than Israel. Israel carried one stream. Greece carried another. Rome would soon provide a third. And in the first century, those streams suddenly meet.

Jesus appears at the intersection.

HELD OPEN AND PROVOCATIVE · NOT PRESENTED AS PROOF
08 VOICES OF THE TRADITION

Three voices on the Logos — in creation, in the incarnation, and in the meaning of every created thing.

Justin Martyr
c. AD 150 · ROME

A philosopher before he was a Christian, and the first to argue that genuine truth found among the philosophers ultimately belongs to Christ — because the Logos had been at work throughout humanity.

SOURCE · CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Logos spermatikos — the seed of the Word, scattered through human thought.

His First and Second Apologies make the argument, and his conclusion was startling even then: whoever lived truly and reasonably before Christ belonged to Christ already. He was executed in Rome for refusing to sacrifice.

SUPPORTS THIS LESSON'S THEME · TRUTH MAY PREPARE THE WAY OUTSIDE ISRAEL · JOHN 1:9 · ACTS 17:22–28
Athanasius
c. AD 330 · ALEXANDRIA

The Word who enters creation in the incarnation is the same Word through whom creation exists.

SOURCE · CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

For Athanasius, incarnation is not God's first relationship with creation. It is the Creator entering his own creation.

On the Incarnation reads creation and incarnation as one continuous act of one Logos — which is exactly why, for him, God taking a body is restoration rather than rescue from matter.

Maximus the Confessor
c. AD 630 · CONSTANTINOPLE

Develops a vision in which the many logoi — the inner purposes or meanings of created things — find their unity in the one Logos.

SOURCE · CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Creation is not a pile of disconnected objects. It possesses a deeper coherence, grounded in Christ.

Each thing has its own meaning and purpose; none of them is arbitrary; all of them belong together. It is a demanding idea, and it gives this course its word for where the consciousness shift is heading — coherence.

HE WAS MUTILATED AND EXILED FOR REFUSING AN IMPERIAL COMPROMISE. THE TITLE “CONFESSOR” RECORDS IT.
