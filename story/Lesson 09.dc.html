LESSON 09 COVENANT · RESTRAINT · CONQUEST · FORMATION

Sinai, Conquest, Judges: Covenant and Violence

Israel has escaped Pharaoh. Now it has to become a people. Law gives freedom a shape — but the same biblical story soon moves into conquest, tribal warfare, and some of Scripture's most disturbing violence.

Can revelation be genuinely divine and still come through an ancient moral world?

01 THE STORY

Israel is free. That is where Lesson 08 left the story — and freedom, it turns out, does not automatically create a just society.

EGYPT TAUGHT ISRAEL
what domination looks like
SINAI NOW ASKS
will the rescued become different from the empire that enslaved them?

And before the commandments begin, the covenant reminds Israel who is speaking and what has already happened.

“I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt.”

EXODUS 20:2 · THE FIRST LINE, BEFORE ANY COMMAND

Rescue still comes before requirement.

THE COMMANDMENTS DO NOT BEGIN

“Obey so that I will rescue you.”

THEY BEGIN AFTER RESCUE

“You have been brought out. Now learn another way to live.”

From there the story runs: Sinai → covenant → laws → wilderness → Canaan → the conquest traditions → Judges. And the results are messy. Israel receives structure and does not instantly become mature.

Judges summarizes the whole period in one sentence: “everyone did what was right in their own eyes.”

Structure can guide development. It cannot force maturity.

02 THE WORLD
HISTORICAL FACT

Torah did not appear in a legal vacuum. The ancient Near East had law codes for centuries before Sinai.

HAMMURABI

The Babylonian code, c. 1750 BC, carved on a stele topped by the king receiving authority from a god. Property, injury, marriage, debt, restitution — graded by social rank.

HITTITE AND ASSYRIAN LAW

Whole libraries of ancient Near Eastern law — Hittite statutes, Middle Assyrian rulings, Ur-Nammu and Lipit-Ishtar before them. Legal culture was old and shared.

TREATY FORM

Sinai's covenant follows the shape of a suzerain treaty: identification of the great king, history of what he has done, stipulations, witnesses, blessings and curses. Israel borrowed the diplomatic form of its era.

WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS

Israel's emergence in Canaan is one of the most argued questions in biblical archaeology — invasion, gradual infiltration, internal social change, or some combination. The narrative and modern reconstruction should not be quietly treated as the same thing.

Biblical law shares forms and assumptions with that world. Comparison is not a threat — it is how we can see where Torah bends the language of its era toward something else.

SABBATH FOR EVERYONE
Rest extended past the elite to servants, resident foreigners and working animals. Exodus 20:8–11
WIDOW · ORPHAN · STRANGER
A repeated triad of the legally defenseless, protected by name. Exodus 22:21–24
DEBT AND RELEASE
Limits on how long a debt could hold a person, and provision for release. Deuteronomy 15:12–15
A CEILING ON REVENGE
One eye, not a village. Exodus 21:23–25
MEMORY AS MOTIVE
“Remember that you were a slave in Egypt” — ethics grounded in experience, not rank. Leviticus 19:33–34

Torah speaks an ancient moral language — and sometimes bends that language toward something new.

Keep the comparison honest in both directions. Israel is not uniquely progressive in every category; Hammurabi also protects widows, and some biblical penalties are harsher than their neighbors'. The movement is real and uneven.

◆ HOW CONSCIOUSNESS GROWS

The course has been assuming this shape since Lesson 03. Here it is in full, because Torah is where it has to be argued rather than implied.

STAGE 1
Meet us where we are

God enters a world people already understand — and that world has its own furniture.

tribe sacrifice patriarchy hierarchy retaliation purity covenant
STAGE 2
Give us structure

External boundaries begin forming behavior before anyone has internalized why.

do not murder · do not steal
protect the vulnerable · limit retaliation
remember the slave · practice Sabbath
STAGE 3
Internalize the value

The prophets will eventually ask whether all this ritual and law has actually produced justice, mercy and faithfulness — or only compliance. Micah 6:8 is that question in one verse.

STAGE 4
Lead us beyond the structure

Jesus pushes external commands inward. Matthew 5 is the clearest sustained example.

murder → anger
adultery → desire
neighbor → enemy
retaliation → nonretaliation
purity boundary → mercy
EXTERNAL AUTHORITY → PRACTICE → INTERNALIZED WISDOM → LOVE

The structure is not necessarily the destination. Sometimes it is the container that makes the next stage possible.

WHY START WITH RULES?

A young child first learns do not hit, and obeys at first because an authority said so. Later the deeper value becomes internal: another person has dignity, and I do not want to harm them.

The rule has succeeded most fully when its purpose has become character.

External command can be a beginning rather than an endpoint. But hold the analogy carefully: it does not mean ancient people were intellectually childish, or that we are automatically their moral superiors. Human beings in every period move forward and regress.

THREE STEPS FORWARD, TWO STEPS BACK · THE COURSE'S FRAME

“An eye for an eye”

It sounds brutal to modern ears. But lex talionis has to be read against what it replaced. In a world where revenge could escalate without limit, proportional retaliation functions as a ceiling: no more than the injury received.

Lesson 06 gave us the escalation this law interrupts.

“Eye for eye” may look violent compared with Jesus. It can look restraining compared with Lamech.

Slavery

Torah regulates slavery. It does not abolish the institution. That creates a serious theological question, and a shallow answer would be worse than none: if God is leading humanity, why not simply prohibit it?

HISTORICAL OBSERVATION

Slavery and bonded labor were structural to every ancient economy. There was no ancient society in the region without some form of it, and debt was the usual road in.

MORAL DEVELOPMENT

Some laws limit treatment, cap the term of Israelite debt servitude, require release with provision, and tie the whole subject back to memory of Egypt. Movement inside an institution is still movement.

THEOLOGICAL PROBLEM

Regulation is not abolition. And Christians eventually used biblical texts on both sides — to defend slavery and to destroy it. The same pages, read two ways, for centuries.

Could a law improve an existing institution without representing God's final desire for that institution?

Purity is not the same as goodness

CLEAN / UNCLEAN

A category about sacred space, community boundaries and embodied life. Childbirth, bodily fluids, corpses and certain foods all make a person ritually unclean — temporarily, and often through something entirely ordinary or good.

GOOD / EVIL

A moral category. Purity language should not automatically be heard as God declaring ordinary bodies morally disgusting — that reading has done real damage.

And plant this now, because it pays off later: Jesus will touch people and conditions associated with impurity — and rather than becoming contaminated, healing seems to move outward from him.

03 WORDS THAT MATTER
תּוֹרָה
TORAH — INSTRUCTION
Usually translated law. Its range also includes teaching, direction, instruction.

The root has to do with pointing or showing the way. Torah is what a parent or teacher gives, not only what a court enforces.

Thinking of it only as a legal code makes the whole lesson harder to see. A code regulates. Instruction forms a people.

חֶסֶד
HESED — STEADFAST LOVE
Loyal love, mercy, steadfastness, covenant faithfulness. No single English word carries it.
CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Hesed is what covenant partners owe each other and what God keeps offering when Israel does not. Translators reach for “lovingkindness,” “mercy,” “steadfast love” — all partial.

It becomes the word the prophets use when they ask what covenant faithfulness actually looks like in practice. Micah 6:8 uses it.

חֵרֶם
HEREM — DEVOTED TO DESTRUCTION
Something placed under a ban — set apart for God by being destroyed. It appears throughout the conquest traditions.
CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

These are among the most morally difficult texts in the Bible, and herem is the word at their center. Deuteronomy 20:16–18 commands it; Joshua 6 narrates it.

Do not soften what the texts say. The interpretive work comes after reading them plainly, not instead of it.

04 CONQUEST

Joshua contains texts in which Israel is commanded to destroy Canaanite populations. The language is sweeping — “left no survivor”, “utterly destroyed.” Christians should not pretend these texts are easy.

INTERPRETATION · GENUINELY UNRESOLVED
01 · HISTORICAL / LITERARY HYPERBOLE

Ancient Near Eastern war accounts routinely used totalizing victory language that did not function as statistical reporting. Kings who claimed annihilation were often describing a defeated army, not an emptied land.

Joshua itself later describes populations still living in areas earlier said to be completely destroyed — “there remains yet very much land to be possessed.” That tension is in the text, not imported into it.

02 · DIVINE JUDGMENT

Traditional readings take the conquest as a unique divine judgment on Canaanite societies. This preserves the text's own theological claim — and leaves the hardest moral questions standing, particularly about noncombatants and children.

03 · ACCOMMODATION / DEVELOPMENT

Israel experienced God inside an ancient world where gods, land, warfare and tribal survival were bound together. The text may preserve a genuine encounter and the moral imagination through which that encounter was understood.

04 · CHRIST-CENTERED READING

Christians eventually have to place conquest imagery beside Jesus: love your enemies · put away your sword · Father, forgive them.

If Jesus is the clearest image of the invisible God, how should Christians read commands attributed to God that appear radically unlike Jesus?

Four lenses, no forced resolution. A group that leaves this section comfortable has been given an answer instead of the texts.

◇ THE PROJECTION QUESTION · RETURNS

When every ancient nation believed its god fought for its tribe, how do we distinguish revelation from projection in Israel's war stories?

Lesson 02 showed how tightly the ancient world bound god, tribe, land, king and military victory together. Israel emerges inside that world, not beside it.

Does Israel simply baptize ordinary tribal warfare?

Does God genuinely work within that framework while leading beyond it?

Are both realities present in the biblical record?

A genuine encounter with God does not guarantee a complete understanding of God.

ONE OF THE MAJOR THEOLOGICAL PRINCIPLES OF THIS COURSE. IT WILL BE NEEDED AGAIN AT KINGSHIP, JUDGMENT, HELL AND EMPIRE.
ACCOMMODATION → FORMATION → CONSEQUENCE
Accommodation

God meets a recently liberated ancient people inside the structures and moral categories actually available to them — treaty forms, sacrifice, tribal law, patriarchal households.

Formation

Torah, covenant, ritual and boundaries become scaffolding for a people learning another way of living together.

Consequence

And Judges shows human freedom producing cycles: distortion, consequences, suffering, awakening, deliverance — and then the whole thing again, a little worse.

Reality can become a teacher when instruction is resisted.

Which is not the same as saying suffering is good, or that God arranges every painful event in order to educate people. The claim is narrower: choices create worlds, and those worlds eventually expose the inadequacy of the consciousness that produced them.

God does not have to eliminate freedom in order to keep leading.

05 JUDGES · THE SYSTEM BREAKS DOWN

Judges is not a collection of heroic stories. It is deliberately disturbing, and it is built as a cycle.

Israel turns away
oppression
cry
a deliverer
temporary peace
repeat — and each turn of the wheel is worse

The cycles deteriorate and the judges themselves become increasingly compromised. By the end of the book: sexual brutality, tribal civil war, moral collapse. The refrain arrives twice, like a verdict.

“Everyone did what was right in their own eyes.”

JUDGES 21:25

The Bible does not hide the failure of its own people.

Which is worth noticing as a fact about this literature. National literatures usually flatter the nation. Israel's repeatedly indicts it — and keeps the indictments in the canon.

06 THE CONSCIOUSNESS SHIFT
FROM
Unlimited retaliation and tribal survival
THROUGH
Covenant, boundaries, proportion, communal justice
TOWARD
Internalized mercy and love

Sinai is not the Sermon on the Mount. But neither is it Lamech's seventy-sevenfold revenge. Moral development may genuinely occur through stages.

Restraint is progress without being the destination.

And a new vision does not instantly erase an old one. Stages coexist.

GRACE BEFORE LAW · RETURNS

Do not let the difficulty of Torah erase the order of Exodus. The sentence about rescue comes first, and everything else is spoken to people who have already been brought out.

FIRST
“I am YHWH your God, who brought you out of Egypt…”
ONLY THEN
“You shall…”

Identity precedes instruction.

I don't have to do to get. I already have it, so I get to do.

The law is not meant to purchase liberation. It is meant to form liberated people.

07 THE CHRIST CONNECTION

It is a Jewish teacher interpreting Israel's Torah from inside Israel's tradition — and pushing each command toward its deepest internal purpose.

FULFILLED, NOT DISCARDED · THE DISTINCTION MATTERS
08 THE TENSION

Can a law be genuinely from God and still not represent God's final moral destination?

PERFECT DIVINE LEGISLATION

A traditional approach treats Torah as precisely appropriate divine law for Israel's covenant context — even where particular commands were never intended for later Christians.

Its strength: it takes the text's own authority seriously and does not make God's word a draft.

ACCOMMODATION AND DEVELOPMENT

Another approach sees God working through culturally available institutions — limiting harm and forming people toward values that only become clear later.

Its strength: it can read the hardest texts without either defending or discarding them.

Could both contain truth? Could a law be genuinely formative at one historical stage without being the final expression of love?

Does revelation have to be timelessly complete at every stage in order to be genuinely revelation?

09 VOICES OF THE TRADITION

Christians have been troubled by these texts since the second century. That is worth saying out loud: the discomfort in the room is not a modern invention.

Origen
c. AD 185–253 · ALEXANDRIA AND CAESAREA

Struggled openly with the violent texts, and read Israel's wars spiritually — as the struggle against sin and destructive powers rather than a pattern for Christians to imitate.

SOURCE · CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Scripture must ultimately be read in a way worthy of Christ.

Origen preached through Joshua and would not let a congregation take it as a military model. The risk in his method is obvious — allegory can dissolve anything — but the instinct behind it is the same one this lesson is working with: Christ is the interpretive center. Lesson 28 returns to him properly.

Gregory of Nyssa
c. AD 335–395 · CAPPADOCIA

Understands human life as continual movement toward God rather than a fixed moral condition — which is what makes a developmental reading of Torah thinkable at all.

SOURCE · CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Formation is dynamic. Growth is not a phase before arrival — it is the shape of the life.

Gregory also read Moses' ascent as a pattern rather than a biography, which is why he keeps appearing in this course wherever development is the question.

Augustine
AD 354–430 · HIPPO

Develops influential arguments about authority, love, coercion and when force might serve justice — the seedbed of what becomes just-war thinking.

SOURCE · CLICK TO LEARN MORE →

Christians will keep wrestling with whether violence can ever serve justice.

The full theory belongs to Lesson 31, not here. But notice the timing: Augustine writes after the church has acquired imperial power, which is exactly when a Christian account of legitimate force becomes urgently useful — and dangerous.

TAKEAWAY

Torah gives freedom a structure — but structure is meant to form people toward something deeper.

Restraint can become justice. Justice can become mercy. External law can become love written on the heart.

The structure is not necessarily the destination. Sometimes it is the container that makes the next stage possible.

WHERE THIS GOES NEXT

Torah gives Israel structure. Judges shows how easily the structure collapses. And Israel's next solution will sound familiar to anyone who has read Exodus: give us a king like the other nations.

What happens when a people rescued from Pharaoh decides it wants a throne?
